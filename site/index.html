01 · Where agents stall

Software is fluent online. Then it reaches the curb.

Agents can browse, compare and fill a cart. They stop at the parts of commerce that assume a person is there:

  1. PickupAn order that has to be collected from a counter.
  2. VoiceA phone or voice step before an order is released.
  3. ChecksA human-verification challenge in the middle of checkout.

And even when checkout works, an operator needs to know the agent didn’t spend more, or somewhere else, than it was allowed to.

02 · How an order moves

Calculate first. Approve the exact number. Keep a record you can inspect.

  1. 1

    Intent

    The agent states what it wants to buy, from where, and why.

  2. 2

    Policy review

    Checked against your rules before any checkout: spending cap, allowed merchants, retry limit.

  3. 3

    Authorization

    Approval covers one merchant, one set of items, one amount. Change any of them and it no longer applies.

  4. 4

    Inspectable receipt

    Tamper-evident signed receipts (post-quantum signing in development).

    04 · For runners

    A masked runner clears the step. The buyer stays private.

    When an order needs a person — collecting from a counter, answering a voice prompt — Cartbop is designed to post a narrow task to a nearby runner. The task carries what’s needed to finish that step and nothing about who is buying.

    The runner network is not recruited yet. Joining the waitlist is how that starts.
